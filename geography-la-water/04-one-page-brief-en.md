# One-Page Brief (for faculty feedback)
## Where Does L.A.’s Water Come From?

**Kai Nozawa · Independent public geography project (not for course credit)**  
**Consultation requested:** informal advice from Dr. Jing Liu

### Question
How is Los Angeles’s water supply spatially produced through local sources and long-distance interbasin transfers, and what does that geography mean for drought/climate resilience?

### Why it matters
Tap water hides a continental-scale infrastructure story. A clear public map story can improve geographic literacy while remaining transparent about methods and limits.

### Scope (v1)
- Narrative home: Los Angeles tap  
- Corridors: LA Aqueduct (Eastern Sierra), Colorado River supply, State Water Project  
- Local: groundwater, recycling, capture (selected)  
- Frame: supply-side resilience (not flood inundation mapping)

### Methods
Public agency sources + open geospatial layers; StoryMap sequence; restrained basemaps; explicit uncertainty notes; bilingual public presentation.

### Deliverables
1. Published StoryMap URL  
2. Methods/Sources section  
3. Full prospectus (EN/JA)

### Feedback requested
1. Is this scope right for a strong independent project?  
2. Which source datasets are most trustworthy for public synthesis?  
3. Where does simplification become misleading?  
4. What should Phase 2 prioritize?
